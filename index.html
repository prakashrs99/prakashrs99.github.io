A little about me

Science at the interface
of synthesis and sustainability

My work focuses on developing practical and selective chemical transformations, with an emphasis on catalysis and the synthesis of complex molecules.

I am a chemist working in synthetic methodology, photoredox catalysis, and functionalization of organic molecules. My doctoral research at the University of Antwerp focused on photoredox catalysis and late-stage functionalization using homogeneous and heterogeneous photocatalytic systems.

I am currently a Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Alberta, where my research includes nucleoside chemistry and C–H bond functionalization of nucleoside analogues for medicinal-chemistry applications.

I am interested in research strategies that combine mechanistic insight, experimental practicality, and more sustainable approaches to molecular synthesis.

Research interests

What I work on

Research directions connecting catalyst design, reaction discovery, and the functionalization of valuable molecular scaffolds.

01 / Catalysis

Photoredox catalysis

Developing light-driven strategies for selective bond formation and functional-group transformations under mild conditions.

02 / Materials

Heterogeneous photocatalysts

Designing solid photocatalytic systems, including metal-modified graphitic carbon nitride, for sustainable organic transformations.

03 / Molecules

Late-stage & C–H functionalization

Building methods to modify complex molecules and nucleoside analogues, with potential relevance to medicinal chemistry.
